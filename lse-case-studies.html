Case Study: Financial Statement Group Project

The first live use of ADA (then known as CARA) involved redesigning the marking criteria for a financial accounting group project at LSE. The course leader, Dr Saipriya Kamath, was concerned that students were over-relying on AI tools to complete their financial analysis.

The challenge

The group project required students to analyse financial statements over 3 to 5 years around significant events. The course leader noticed that while students submitted polished reports, their performance on in-person exam questions requiring the same analytical skills had deteriorated. This suggested that reports were being outsourced to AI.

Working with ADA

Dr Kamath came with a general idea and through structured conversation with ADA, the session progressed through key decision points.

1

Exploring implementation options

They chose to add a dedicated AI Baseline Comparison criterion alongside an evidence trail requirement.

2

Defining independent analysis

Students would identify what the AI missed or oversimplified, then demonstrate how their own analysis addressed these shortcomings.

3

Addressing fairness

Students who chose not to use AI would be provided with an exemplar AI analysis upon request.

4

Resolving the originality question

ADA’s structured questioning helped the course leader realise that “originality” and “creativity” were problematic terms. She removed the creativity criterion entirely.

5

Determining weighting

Content & Knowledge (25%), AI Baseline Comparison (25%), Research Skills (20%), Analytical Workings (20%), Organisation (10%).

The outcome

“It was surprisingly good. I came with a very general idea... and I was able to have a conversation with the agent. The agent gave interesting directions to take, but also asked some piercing questions which made me think of how exactly I wanted to proceed. The process only took about an hour and a half.”Dr Saipriya Kamath, Department of Accounting, LSE

Wider Adoption at LSE

ADA has been applied across LSE departments, by the school-wide oral assessment working group, and by the PGCertHE cohort.
