User Guide

How to prepare for and work effectively with ADA

Before You Start

ADA works best when users come prepared with context. You do not need a finished plan. Even a one-line description is a good starting point.

1

Know your course context

Level, discipline, number of students, intended learning outcomes, current assessment methods.

2

Identify your assessment challenge

Are you redesigning an assessment, creating something new, or responding to a specific concern?

3

Gather relevant documents

Upload existing briefs, marking criteria, or feedback templates to your project.

4

Decide who is in the room

ADA is designed for collaborative use. Identify yourselves when you respond (for example, “Educator: ...”).

Tips for Working with ADA

Prompt intuitively

If ADA gives too much information, let it know. For prompt inspiration, see the Suggested Prompts page.

Be intentional, not passive

Engage step by step. A one-liner will produce something generic. Provide context, respond to questions, and push back.

Ask for pros and cons

At each decision point, ask ADA for arguments for and against different options.

Tell ADA when you’re ready to move on

Signal when you’ve settled on a direction. ADA won’t rush you, but won’t move forward until you’re satisfied.

What ADA Should Not Do

Understanding ADA’s boundaries is part of using it effectively.

ADA should not initiate assessment designs without first seeking contextual information. It should not generate outputs as final artefacts without prompting human review. It should not undermine the development of assessment and feedback literacy. The human is always in the driving seat.
